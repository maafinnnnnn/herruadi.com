## 📄 Page 2 — About

### Section 1 — Who I Am

```
I started as a frontend developer — close enough to design
to fall in love with it, far enough to understand how
things get built.

The switch wasn't planned. But once I crossed over,
I never looked back.

Nine years later, what keeps me here isn't the craft —
it's the impact. A product I designed helping a kitchen
run smoother. An app that actually solves someone's real
problem. An agency that lands a client because the work
looked and felt right. Small ripples, maybe. But ripples matter.

I live by one principle: hidup harus berguna bagi sesama —
life should be useful to others. It's why I design, why I'm
honest with clients even when it's uncomfortable, and why
I named my son Gunaka Sasama — Sundanese for "useful to others."

That's not a tagline. That's just who I am.
```

---

### Section 2 — How I Work

```
My process starts with one question: is this actually the problem?

Before I open Figma, I want to understand what we're really
solving. I break down the problem, challenge the assumptions,
and make sure we're building the right thing — not just
building things right. If something doesn't make sense,
we talk it through. Designing the wrong solution is just
expensive guesswork.

That said — I know when to lead and when to follow.

When there's a PM or PO with a solid PRD on the table,
I'm heads down and executing with precision. I respect process,
I respect structure, and I know how to work within a system
without losing the quality of the output.

Two modes. One standard: make it count.
```

---

### Section 3 — The Human Side

```
Outside of work, I'm a dad who does the school run every
morning for my 4-year-old — which, honestly, is the best
part of my day.

I decompress on two wheels, exploring Bandung's streets
or finding roads I've never taken before.
I keep telling myself I'll pick up music again soon.

The bigger dream? A design studio and a free learning space
for people who want to build in tech but can't afford
the privilege of education. Not someday — just not today yet.
```
